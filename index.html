Hazard Gentrification & Environmental Justice

/> />

This project evaluates how historical redlining spatially aligns with race, income, education, and natural hazard exposure across Seattle, Washington.

The research investigates whether low-income and minority populations are disproportionately distributed in natural hazard zones and whether those communities may face increased risk of hazard gentrification and climate-driven displacement.

ACS demographic estimates, historical HOLC redlining data, and Seattle hazard exposure datasets were integrated within a GIS workflow to analyze spatial patterns of vulnerability, inequality, and neighborhood change.

The project also examines demographic shifts between ACS 5-year estimates to identify potential indicators of gentrification within historically redlined communities.

Additionally, hazard exposure data is obtained from the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA)'s National Risk Index database. This database assesses the risk of 18 natural hazards across the United States.

LiDAR-Based Landslide Susceptibility Assessment

This project visualizes landslide susceptibility across Mercer Island using LiDAR-derived DEMs, slope analysis, hillshade modeling, and terrain classification techniques.

Multiple terrain models revealed elevated landslide risk near the I-90 corridor and the outer slopes of Mercer Island.

Together, the models demonstrate how LiDAR terrain visualization supports hazard mitigation planning and geospatial risk assessment.
